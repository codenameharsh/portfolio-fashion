PACE / 01 ★

Independent Technical Apparel Development Project

Three pieces.
One running system.

PACE is a women’s performance-running capsule designed as a layered system for movement, ventilation, storage, and lightweight coverage.

Women’s Performance Running
Role
Apparel Design · Technical Design · Product Development
Range

At a glance

Role: Apparel + technical design

Focus: Movement, ventilation, layering

Outcome: Three-piece development system

OverviewResearchDirectionMaterialsGarmentsDevelopment

01 / Overview

Designed for low-interruption movement.

Rather than treating each garment as an isolated design, I developed PACE as a cohesive layering system. Curved construction lines, restrained branding, lightweight materials, and purposeful details create a common language across all three pieces.

The capsule is designed around the recreational runner who moves between running, walking, light training, and daily activity. The goal was commercially approachable performance apparel, not specialized race equipment.

02 / Research

The recreational runner.

PACE is considered for women who run 3–5 times a week and use activewear beyond dedicated training. Their wardrobe moves from running → walking → light training → daily activity.

Freedom of movementLightweight constructionVentilationSecure small-item storageComfortable layeringPractical versatility

04 / Materials

Proposed sourcing, clearly framed.

Each material callout is a proposed project specification, not a verified supplier, lab, or production claim. The tee pairs fine-gauge performance jersey with stretch mesh; the short uses a lightweight stretch-woven shell with a stretch-knit liner; the shell explores a matte stretch woven/ripstop with mesh ventilation.

Final material composition, GSM, finish, performance, and color standards would require supplier confirmation and physical testing.

05 / P01-RT

PACE Air Tee

Lightweight ventilation without compression.

Design response

A semi-fitted silhouette follows the body without becoming compression-oriented. The curved hem gives subtle visual movement and added back coverage.

Key details

  • Shallow crew neckline
  • Set-in sleeve with forward shoulder seam
  • Mesh side ventilation panel
  • Centered rear reflective transfer

05 / P02-RS

PACE 4" Run Short

Movement, support, and low-bulk storage.

Technical centerpiece

The short brings fit, waistband construction, mobility, liner, and storage into a single product. A 4–5 cm curved side split supports stride without relying solely on stretch.

Key details

  • 4 cm contoured waistband
  • Internal flat drawcord
  • Concealed center-back zip pocket
  • Close-fitting brief liner

06 / P03-LJ

PACE Air Shell

Lightweight coverage with protected ventilation.

Layer + protect

The shell shifts to a raglan sleeve and regular athletic fit for layering ease. An overlapping upper-back yoke covers a mesh vent, balancing exterior coverage with passive ventilation.

Key details

  • Low stand collar with reverse-coil zip
  • Angled zip pockets with mesh bags
  • Overlapping back vent
  • Partially elasticated cuff and internal hem drawcord

07 / Technical development

Design intent → documentation → validation

Technical flats, construction callouts, proposed material specifications, POMs, grading logic, and manufacturer-facing documentation translate the visual direction into a development-ready starting point.

Design intentTech packProto sampleMeasure + fitFit commentsRevised specProto 2Size set

08 / Final capsule

One system, three responses to performance.

Air Tee

Targeted ventilation + lightweight next-to-skin comfort

4" Run Short

Mobility + integrated support + secure storage

Air Shell

Layering + protected ventilation + lightweight coverage

PACE is an educational concept-development project. No physical samples, laboratory tests, supplier confirmations, or production results have been completed. Specifications shown are proposed starting points for future development and validation.

Key takeaway: three garments respond to performance needs without repeating the same solution.

AURA / 02

Independent Fashion Design & Brand Project

Where dreams
weave light.

AURA translates an abstract celestial identity into a luxury womenswear collection: from atmosphere and silhouettes to illustration, editorial direction, packaging, and digital touchpoints.

At a glance

Role: Fashion design + creative direction

Focus: Silhouette, illustration, identity

Outcome: Collection + luxury brand world

01 / Overview

A brand and collection, designed as one system.

AURA began with the brand rather than an individual garment. I developed the visual identity, fashion direction, original illustrations, collection language, and editorial presentation as interconnected parts of one experience.

The project asks how identity can influence the design language of a collection—so that typography, photography, styling, color, and silhouette feel as though they belong to the same visual universe.

02 / Creative direction

A celestial vision with contemporary restraint.

Fluidity, elegance, contrast, individuality, and editorial storytelling form the core of Aura. Soft movement is balanced with stronger graphic elements; distinct silhouettes retain a feeling of refinement rather than excess.

03 / Color direction

Color as collection architecture.

Midnight navy, velvet noir, cerulean mist, sunlit cream, and golden honey create contrast between night-sky depth and luminous warmth. The palette connects garments, illustrations, and brand applications.

Midnight SkySunlit CreamGolden HoneyCerulean MistVelvet Noir

04 / Fashion illustration

Illustration as a collection tool.

Silhouette, color, styling, and proportion were developed together, allowing the final fashion work to carry the same graceful, dreamlike quality established through the brand direction.

05 / Building the collection

From reference to gesture to refined design.

Individual looks were evaluated in relation to silhouette, color, proportion, styling, and visual rhythm. The resulting collection uses variation between statement and supporting pieces while maintaining a recognizable Aura identity.

06 / Brand identity

A visual language for every touchpoint.

The collection’s visual language extends into a starborn logo system, editorial typography, rich color, digital art direction, and packaging. The goal was not simply to decorate the collection, but to make each application feel part of the same world.

07 / Digital constellation

Luxury translated to the screen.

Editorial spacing, moody imagery, warm metallic accents, and quiet navigation translate Aura’s identity into an immersive digital retail experience.

08 / Outcome

Fashion world-building across design and image.

AURA demonstrates a process that moves from research to creative direction, silhouette, fashion illustration, collection development, visual identity, and editorial presentation—bringing fashion and visual design into one coherent luxury concept.

Key takeaway: a luxury collection feels more coherent when the clothes and the visual identity are designed as one system.

MADRAS MERCH / 03

Independent Apparel Graphics Project

Wear your
Chennai.

Madras Merch turns the city’s everyday culture, people, places, and expressions into original apparel graphics and merchandise.

8 Original designs850+ SalesApparel · journals · framed artwork
At a glance

Role: Cultural research + graphic design

Focus: Chennai references made wearable

Outcome: 8 designs · 850+ sales

01 / Opportunity

Chennai beyond the postcard.

Rather than relying on generic city iconography, Madras Merch began with a question: what makes Chennai recognizable to the people who actually know it?

The project translated familiar cultural references into original graphics designed to be wearable, visually distinct, and commercially relevant across apparel and lifestyle products.

02 / Cultural research

Observe → identify → interpret.

Research moved across local language, food, transport, architecture, popular culture, everyday objects, nostalgia, and neighborhood identity. The goal was not simply to collect icons, but to find references that could be abstracted and reinterpreted while remaining recognizable.

Food + local flavoursTransport + street lifeArchitecture + landmarksLanguage + expressionArt + traditionEveryday moments

03 / From culture to graphic

Reference → sketch → composition → final artwork.

Each graphic was developed through illustration, typography, symbol, composition, color, and local reference. The central challenge was balancing specificity with legibility: too literal, and it becomes souvenir merchandise; too abstract, and the cultural connection disappears.

04 / The collection

Eight original designs.

Each artwork carries a distinct cultural reference, while the collection stays cohesive through bold color, expressive type, and illustration-led composition.

05 / Designing for apparel

Artwork that works beyond the screen.

Every selected graphic was considered for print scale, placement, garment color, contrast, viewing distance, composition, and print boundaries. This made the final applications feel designed for apparel rather than simply placed onto a mockup.

  • Filter Kaapi: Centered, larger print preserves the illustrative detail.
  • Madras Map: Works as an expansive back graphic with room for fine linework.
  • Type-led graphics: High contrast colorways stay legible at distance.

06 / Commercial outcome

8Original designs
850+Sales across apparel, journals, and framed merchandise

The commercial response showed that culturally specific design could remain visually distinctive while connecting with a broader merchandise audience.

07 / Reflection

Specificity became the collection’s strength.

Madras Merch reinforced that an apparel graphic is not only an illustration. It must work within the constraints of placement, scale, color, production method, garment context, and customer recognition. The project brought together fashion, culture, illustration, graphic design, and commercial product thinking.

Key takeaway: culturally specific graphics became the collection’s strongest commercial differentiator.

About

Technical by training.
Visual by instinct.

I’m Harshitha Rajendran, a multidisciplinary designer with a foundation in Fashion Technology and experience across apparel, brand, visual, and digital product design.

My work sits at the intersection of how products are designed, made, experienced, and communicated. I began with garment construction, apparel production, quality, and fashion, then expanded into branding, UI/UX, ecommerce, and visual systems.

I’m particularly interested in apparel product development and technical design, where creative intent has to translate into clear, measurable information. My work includes technical flats, garment specifications, POMs, construction details, tech packs, fashion illustration, apparel graphics, and product storytelling.

My current focus is on apparel design, technical design, product development, design operations, and product integrity—roles where I can combine fashion knowledge with visual and systems thinking.
